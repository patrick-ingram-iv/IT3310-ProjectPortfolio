## Estimation Approach

I used bottom-up estimating by identifying the work required for each role and estimating the weeks and hours needed. The Week 8 schedule guided the staffing estimates. Since actual NovaTech labor rates and staffing commitments were not provided, I used reasonable hourly cost assumptions.

**Assumption 1:** Team members will be available for the estimated weekly hours. If availability decreases, the project may require additional time or replacement resources, increasing costs.

**Assumption 2:** Development, testing, and training can be completed within the estimated effort. If defects require rework or staff need additional training, labor hours and costs will increase.

These are planning estimates, and actual costs may vary.

The reserve is based on the estimated additional work needed if these two high-priority Week 9 risks require responses. It is not an automatic percentage of the project budget. Actual exposure could differ, and the reserve does not cover every possible cost associated with all five risks.

## Budget Tradeoff Analysis

The Sponsor has requested consideration of a 10% reduction in the estimated budget without changing the approved project outcome. A 10% reduction equals **$8,424**, resulting in a proposed revised budget of **$75,816**.

Before agreeing to the reduction, I would review labor allocations, software costs, and the schedule to identify realistic savings without compromising approved requirements.

**Tradeoff or Risk 1:** Reducing developer or QA hours could lower labor costs, but it could delay testing, leave defects unresolved, or increase the risk of incorrect appointment records.

**Tradeoff or Risk 2:** Reducing training resources could save money initially, but clinic staff may be unprepared to use the system, increasing scheduling errors and support costs.

I would first investigate existing software licenses, shared resources, and more efficient scheduling. I would not recommend reducing critical testing, data validation, or staff training solely to meet the budget target.

The revised budget is a proposed target, not a validated lower-cost plan. I would support the reduction only after confirming that the savings are achievable without changing the approved project outcome.
