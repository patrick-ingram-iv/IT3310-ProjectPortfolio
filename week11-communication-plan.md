### Communication Method Justification

**Entry 1 — Clinic Intake Coordinators (Interactive)**

I chose Interactive communication for the Clinic Intake Coordinators because they use the scheduling system every day. They need to understand workflow changes, receive proper training, and have opportunities to ask questions and report problems. In my Week 4 Stakeholder Register, I classified them as high interest and low influence, with a Keep Informed strategy. Biweekly meetings allow NovaTech to receive feedback directly from the people using the system. Urgent scheduling or training issues would require additional communication rather than waiting for the next meeting.

**Entry 4 — RCHN Board of Directors (Push)**

I chose Push communication for the RCHN Board of Directors because they need regular updates on project progress, budget, major risks, and decisions requiring approval without being involved in daily operations. My Week 4 Stakeholder Register classified the Board as low interest and high influence, with a Keep Satisfied strategy. Monthly executive summaries provide the information they need without unnecessary technical details. However, significant risks, budget changes, or decisions requiring immediate attention should be communicated as they arise rather than waiting for the monthly report.

## Escalation Path

**Level 1 — NovaTech Technical Lead / Project Team**

Handles routine technical issues, testing problems, and scheduling discrepancies. Escalation begins when an issue cannot be resolved during normal project work or could affect an upcoming milestone.

Expected response: Within 4 business hours (planning recommendation).

**Level 2 — NovaTech Project Manager**

Handles unresolved technical issues, staff training delays, resource conflicts, and risks affecting the project schedule or budget. Escalation occurs when the project team cannot resolve the problem or a management decision is required.

Expected response: Within 1 business day (planning recommendation).

**Level 3 — RCHN Project Sponsor / Executive Leadership**

Handles major risks affecting patient care, significant budget or schedule changes, and decisions beyond the Project Manager's authority. Escalation occurs when executive approval is required or unresolved issues could jeopardize a safe rollout.

Expected response: Within 1 business day, immediately for urgent patient safety concerns (planning recommendation).

### Escalation Scenario

If the appointment-data discrepancies identified in my Week 9 Decision Record turned out to involve incorrect patient bookings, I would follow the escalation path established in the Communication Plan.

**Level 1:** The data migration and technical teams would investigate the discrepancies, identify affected records, and determine whether the errors could be corrected. The initial response would occur within four business hours, with immediate notification if patient care were at risk.

**Level 2:** If the errors could not be resolved or threatened the deployment schedule, the NovaTech Project Manager would review the findings, coordinate corrective actions, and assess the impact on testing and clinic operations. The expected response would be within one business day.

**Level 3:** If the problem could affect patient care or require a change to the rollout decision, the Project Manager would immediately escalate it to RCHN Project Sponsor Marcus Webb. Leadership would review the evidence and determine whether to postpone deployment or authorize a conditional phased rollout once the affected clinic met all readiness requirements.

I would not recommend proceeding until appointment accuracy, critical testing, staff readiness, and backup procedures were verified. Protecting patients is more important than meeting an unverified launch date.

## Gap Analysis

### Communication Gap

The gap I identified in my Communication Plan is the lack of a specific communication requirement for clinic managers to confirm staff readiness before deployment. This matters because two of the six clinics still need additional training. Even if the technical work is completed, unprepared staff could make scheduling mistakes that affect patient care. To fix this gap, I would add a communication requirement for clinic managers to report training completion and any remaining concerns to the NovaTech Project Manager before each clinic is approved for rollout.

### Silence as a Communication Decision

A situation where NovaTech's silence could create a problem is during system testing. Even if testing is progressing on schedule, the Clinic Intake Coordinators might assume something is wrong if they receive no updates about the system or upcoming training. This could create confusion and reduce their confidence in the project. My Communication Plan addresses this by scheduling biweekly interactive meetings where employees can receive progress updates, ask questions, and raise concerns. Important changes or problems would be communicated immediately rather than waiting for the next meeting.
